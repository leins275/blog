It's time to talk about the eternal.

Namely, about productivity and task management. My system is constantly changing, so today I want to share the latest updates.



# **Calendar**

The digital calendar remains a constant element, for planned events and meetings.



# **Todoist**

I've tried several times to give up using Todoist as a task list in favor of a notebook. But Todoist is incredibly useful, so I never managed to completely quit it. Todoist is good for capturing tasks both on the go from my phone and at the computer using the hotkey for quick task entry.

Now I've reduced my system to a minimum. I have one project - tasks. And there are tags that denote projects/areas, for context. I don't use anything beyond that. Sometimes there's a quiet period, when I mostly assign tasks to myself. Sometimes it's the opposite, when in twenty minutes 10 new tasks come in that are worth capturing. Todoist acts as such a backlog - a settling tank. Some tasks from the backlog eventually spoil and get deleted, others simply wait for their moment. But I'm confident nothing is forgotten, and that's good.

The main thing is to actually delete unneeded tasks. I have no lists for "someday" and the like. If a task isn't done for a long time, I simply delete it. As a last resort - if it contains some valuable information - I can move it to notes.



# **Notebook**

However, even though I haven't given up on Todoist, I also haven't given up on the notebook. It has a number of advantages, and I'll tell you about them now.



## **System**

Let's start with my system for working with the notebook. GTD describes rituals such as the daily and weekly review. The classic Bullet Journal distinguishes between the Daily Log and the Monthly Log. In this sense, the weekly cadence appeals to me much more. And I always strive for simplification, so I lay out the A5 notebook immediately for both the day and the week. Through trial and error, I established that half a page is optimal for me. So my week takes up two spreads, where the pages are horizontally divided in half. That gives a total of 8 blocks. The very first block I reserve for the overall plans for the week, not tied to a specific day. Let's say it's a checklist of what would be good to get through this week. The remaining blocks are assigned to the days of the week. During the weekly review, I enter events from the electronic calendar for the week, and also outline a checklist of what I want to do overall. During the daily review, I plan tasks for the next day and also log events I consider interesting from today.



## **The Value of the Notebook in My System**


Now about the advantages of the notebook.

First, it's an analog tool. For me, the ability to at least briefly tear away from screens and notifications is very valuable, and the notebook provides that.

Second, if you use a calendar and Todoist, that's already two tools. And to see the full picture of the day, you have to somehow cross them. For me, the notebook is also a way to assemble a convenient agenda into one list that's easy to look at.

Third, unlike Todoist, where reflecting is inconvenient, the notebook gives excellent feedback and an opportunity for logging.
