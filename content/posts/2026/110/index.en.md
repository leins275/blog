An affirmation before a pointless hour-and-a-half morning work call, from Marcus Aurelius:

In the morning one should say to oneself: "Today I will have to deal with people who are meddlesome, ungrateful, arrogant, deceitful, envious, and unsociable. These qualities stem from their ignorance of good and evil. But I, who have come to know the beautiful nature of good and the shameful nature of evil, also understand the nature of those who go astray. They are akin to me not by blood and birth, but by divine dispensation and reason. Knowledge protects me from their evil. They cannot draw me into anything shameful. Yet I must not be angry with or hate those who are akin to me. We were made for cooperation, like feet, hands, eyelids, and the upper and lower rows of teeth. Therefore to oppose one another is contrary to nature; and to be vexed with such people and to shun them is to oppose them."

(c) To Myself. Reflections. Book 2, 1
