I've finally worked out an approach to literature notes. In Sönke Ahrens' terminology, literature notes are the place where you record the essence of some source of information: a book, a video, an article. Essentially, they're summaries. I've tried reading and taking notes in every possible way and on every available device. Now I read only on an iPad. Why? I'll explain.

The iPad is one compact device. The fewer physical items you need for an activity, the fewer constraints there are on your environment. The fewer the constraints, the greater the chance of ending up in a suitable environment. That, in turn, increases the chance that you'll actually read and take notes at all. If you write in a notebook, for example, ideally you need a desk. I already sit at a desk all the time, and I'd like to change my environment sometimes. So I often read sitting on the couch, for example.

The iPad also gives the fastest switching between activities. Often I've just read something and want to write it down right away, and then quickly get back to reading. With the iPad I do this with a single swipe. You can nicely spread out on a desk with papers and books, but then we run into the point about environment.

Why specifically a stylus? Handwritten notes are the most convenient, because you want to make notes quickly in a free-form format. To improve legibility, I write almost everything in block capitals. Also, following the principle of solidity of notes, I keep all notes on one book on a single canvas in Freeform.
